# PO-Guard: Material Purchase Order Review System

**Course:** ENGG 189: Emerging Technologies Final Project  
**Target Users:** Production Planning & Ordering Teams (Manufacturing Facilities)

---

## 1. Project Description

The project is a **Material Purchase Order (PO) Review System** tailored for manufacturing plant operations. Rather than replacing existing enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems or procurement infrastructure, this solution functions as an **intermediate verification layer** that complements the current operational workflow.

In standard industrial practice, purchase orders are initiated and sent directly to department heads, plant managers, or procurement supervisors for sign-off. This system intercepts that pathway: it receives the submitted PO request, conducts an automated pre-evaluation against plant constraints, and attaches a diagnostic assessment flag prior to managerial review.

### Inputs

To perform accurate evaluations, the system aggregates and synthesizes three distinct streams of data:

1. **Current Production Plan:** The live manufacturing schedule detailing scheduled production runs, start dates, and Bill of Materials (BOM) item requirements. This defines dynamic demand over time.
2. **Stock On-Hand & Incoming Stock:** The current physical inventory counts coupled with scheduled vendor shipments, purchase orders in transit, and their respective Estimated Times of Arrival (ETAs). This defines current and projected supply.
3. **Purchase Orders (POs):** The active purchase order requests requiring verification, containing an item, quantity, unit, ETA duration, and supplier.

The primary deliverable for each evaluated PO is an **actionable assessment flag** accompanied by transparent, plain-language reasoning:

* **Recommended:** The order quantity matches a legitimate time-phased deficit. Timing warnings can appear alongside this quantity decision.
* **Redundant / Overstock:** The requested materials are already covered by existing stock or scheduled incoming orders.
* **Adjust Quantity:** The PO quantity exceeds or falls short of the exact net requirement.
* **Timing Conflict / Expedite Needed:** Needed materials miss the target of arriving at least one calendar day before production starts. Show whether this also causes a projected stockout.
* **Potential Duplicate / Covered by Pending or Approved PO:** Another pending or approved PO provisionally covers some or all of the shortage.
* **Arriving Early:** The PO is expected before the first production date that needs its quantity; show that need date and the number of days early.

## 2. Needs Assessment & Operational Value

In active manufacturing environments, production planners face continuous information fragmentation. Manually cross-referencing a new PO against inventory tallies, shipment manifests, and changing shift schedules is labor-intensive and cognitively demanding.

During peak production cycles or schedule shifts, the sheer volume of data leads to two critical failure modes:

1. **Missed Errors & Blind Approvals:** Planners and approving managers under heavy workloads experience review fatigue, inadvertently approving redundant or ill-timed orders.
2. **Incorrect Purchasing:** Erroneous orders tie up working capital in idle stock or trigger unexpected assembly line stoppages when critical parts fail to arrive on time.

The Material Purchase Order Review System alleviates this burden:

* **Targeted Review Focus:** The manager can review routine requests more quickly and focus detailed attention on flagged discrepancies. All POs still require the manager's review and approval before ordering.
* **Elimination of Mental Math:** Replaces manual spreadsheet calculations with time-phased net availability algorithms.
* **Reduced Order Errors:** Catches duplicate orders, overstock scenarios, and lead-time timing mismatches before financial commitments are made.

## 3. Project Scope & Boundary Conditions

* **Requirement Alignment:** Validating whether requested PO quantities match actual material requirements driven by scheduled production runs and active BOMs.
* **Time-Phased Inventory Matching:** Computing stock balance projections based on calculated PO arrival dates, current inventory, and inbound delivery ETAs.
* **Lead-Time & Timing Conflict Detection:** Checking whether needed materials arrive at least one calendar day before production runs.
* **Cumulative Evaluation:** Assessing pending POs cumulatively rather than in isolation to prevent double-ordering for the same deficit.
* **Decision Support & Feedback Loop:** Allowing reviewers to approve or reject POs with mandatory rejection rationales recorded for revision loops.

### Out of Scope

To maintain a tight, achievable technical boundary, the system explicitly excludes the following domains:

* **Facility Storage & Volumetric Constraints:**
  The system **does not** track physical floor space, warehouse square footage, rack utilization, or maximum bin capacity. It assumes that if a material is needed for production, physical plant accommodation is managed externally.

* **Financial & Capital Budgeting:**
  The system **does not** evaluate unit costs, total order value, department spending limits, cash-flow balances, or vendor payment terms. Its primary criterion is operational/production feasibility, not financial authorization.

* **PO Data Completeness & Master Record Validation:**
  The system **does not** perform general clerical audits (e.g., verifying vendor tax IDs, correct billing addresses, or carrier terms). It operates under the assumption that existing plant ERP or procurement forms already guarantee structural record completeness prior to ingestion.

## Project Requirements

### Data and assessment rules

* **PO email:** Each email must contain exactly one item, positive quantity, unit, supplier, and ETA duration in whole days or weeks (one week = seven calendar days). Emails containing multiple items go to the unverified queue with a request to submit a separate email for each item; the system must not silently select or combine items. The system records the sender and receipt time, assigns a PO ID, and maps the item to an item code. Inventory stores item code, on-hand quantity, and unit; incoming deliveries store item code, quantity, unit, ETA, and receipt status; production runs store start date and outstanding BOM quantities and units by item code.
* **Unit consistency:** Each item has one inventory unit. PO, incoming delivery, and BOM quantities must use that same unit. A mismatch shows *Manual Review Required — Unit Mismatch*, identifies the conflicting record and expected unit, and blocks automated classification until corrected. The manager must correct both quantity and unit where necessary; the system does not automatically convert units. Invalid records do not provide provisional coverage or enter a calculation as if their units matched.
* **Inbox review:** Poll at least every 60 seconds and allow manual sync. Complete emails from authorized senders become Pending Review POs. Reprocessing the same email message ID creates no duplicate; a separately sent email creates a new PO. Unauthorized or incomplete emails go to an unverified queue with a reason. An unmapped item requires manual review.
* **Arrival date and supply status:** The supplier's ETA duration starts when the PO is set to Ordered/Incoming. Before ordering, show a provisional arrival date calculated as today's date + ETA duration and recalculate it as the day changes; approval does not start the supplier clock. On ordering, fix the expected arrival as the ordered date + ETA duration. An Approved PO retains provisional coverage but does not become incoming supply. Ordering creates one linked incoming-delivery record; later edits update that record rather than creating another. Count it once. Rejected POs do not count.
* **Projected stock:** For each item code, project the balance at every outstanding production date through the last outstanding run. At each date, add on-hand stock and unreceived deliveries due by that date, then subtract outstanding production demand due by that date. Completed runs and material consumption already reflected in current inventory are excluded. Production records identify remaining material requirements; completing a run removes its outstanding demand, with the manager ensuring current inventory reflects actual consumption. Received deliveries count only in on-hand stock. Evaluate pending POs by receipt time, using PO ID to break ties. Earlier pending or approved POs provide provisional coverage only on production dates when they are expected to have arrived. Approval preserves that coverage and its original priority; ordering replaces it with one linked incoming delivery. Show provisional coverage separately and show balances before and after the evaluated PO.
* **Quantity decision:** Let A be the evaluated PO's expected arrival date and B(t) its projected balance before adding that PO, including existing supply and eligible earlier pending or approved POs. Calculate B(t) at A and at every outstanding production date on or after A. Net requirement = max(0, -min B(t)) over these dates; use the largest cumulative deficit, not the sum of deficits. At A, include outstanding demand due on or before A and supply due on or before A, even when A follows the last scheduled run. Earlier deficits remain timing warnings but do not increase the quantity requirement if other supply has already covered them by A. Mark the PO *Redundant / Overstock* if the requirement is zero, *Recommended* if its quantity matches a positive requirement, or *Adjust Quantity* otherwise. Show the requirement and quantity difference (PO quantity minus requirement). For a positive requirement, if arrival precedes the first outstanding production date needing that quantity, also show *Arriving Early*, that need date, and the number of days early. The no-scheduled-demand rule takes precedence when no outstanding runs exist.
* **Worked shortage example:** With zero stock, no other supply or POs, demand of 50 units on Day 4 and 30 on Day 10 produces balances of -50 and -80. The net requirement is 80 units, not 130. An 80-unit PO arriving on Day 3 produces balances of 30 on Day 4 and 0 on Day 10. Show *Recommended* and *Arriving Early*, with Day 4 as the first need date and one day early; the one-day buffer is satisfied.
* **No scheduled demand:** If an item has no outstanding production runs, show *Manual Review Required — No Scheduled Demand* instead of *Redundant / Overstock*. Do not calculate a net requirement from an empty schedule. When outstanding runs exist, assess through the last outstanding run and show that planning end date, even if it is far in the future.
* **Warnings and arrival buffer:** Materials should arrive at least one calendar day before a production run starts. Show a *Timing Conflict / Expedite Needed* warning when the PO or another needed delivery misses that target, including arrival on the run date; identify any resulting stockout separately. A timing warning can coexist with *Recommended* or *Adjust Quantity*, which assess quantity only. Flag *Potential Duplicate / Covered by Pending or Approved PO* when an earlier pending or approved PO covers some or all of the same shortage; show the covered amount. Show all applicable warnings alongside the quantity decision. Invalid required data or an unmapped item blocks automated classification and shows *Manual Review Required*.
* **Assessment accuracy:** All specified acceptance scenarios must produce the exact expected stock balances, net requirements, quantity differences, quantity decisions, and applicable warnings. Run each scenario from its stated starting data. The system applies the defined calculation rules rather than estimating results with a learned model.
* **Daily ETA refresh:** At the start of each new calendar day in the application's configured plant time zone, advance provisional ETAs for Pending Review and Approved POs using today's date plus supplier lead time, and rerun affected assessments and provisional-coverage calculations. Ordered delivery ETAs remain fixed unless edited. If the application was unavailable at the date change, perform the refresh when it resumes before showing assessments as current.
* **Manager access and data freshness:** The application assumes a single manager as its sole authorized user. All POs pass through this manager for review and approval. Multiple user roles and approval hierarchies are out of scope. The manager can edit inventory, incoming deliveries and ETAs, production runs, and PO statuses. Show when inventory was last updated alongside assessments so the manager can judge the freshness of the input.
* **Correction workflow:** The manager can open an incomplete or unmapped record, inspect its original email and validation reasons, correct required fields or select an existing item code, and save to rerun validation and assessment. Preserve the original email and message ID; update the existing record rather than creating a duplicate. Valid records from authorized senders enter Pending Review. Invalid records retain specific correction messages, and correcting fields does not bypass sender authorization.
* **Allowed status transitions:** Pending Review can become Approved or Rejected; Approved can become Ordered/Incoming or Rejected. Every rejection requires a reason. A manager can revise a Rejected PO and resubmit the same record to Pending Review after validation; preserve the original email, PO ID, receipt time, and rejection history, and rerun assessment. Other transitions are blocked. Ordered/Incoming POs cannot be rejected or moved backward through these controls; delivery receipt is tracked separately. Pending PO details can be corrected; an Approved PO requiring quantity or item revision must first be rejected and resubmitted for review.
* **Receiving exactly once:** Marking an unreceived delivery as received adds its quantity to on-hand stock once and excludes it from incoming supply in the same saved operation. Keep the delivery record visible with its received status. Repeating the action, retrying a request, or refreshing must not add stock again. Once received, its item, quantity, and receipt status cannot be changed through the delivery form; the manager corrects stock discrepancies through inventory editing.

### Example accepted PO email

Each email requests one item. For example:

```text
Subject: PO
Item: ITEM-B
Quantity: 100
Unit: pieces
Supplier: Example Materials Supplier
ETA duration: 5 days
```

The item must map to an inventory record whose unit is pieces. The five-day duration starts when the PO is set to Ordered/Incoming.

| **T-07** | Validate units across PO, inventory, incoming delivery, and BOM records. | Calculations require quantities expressed in the same unit. | Use the inventory item unit throughout.
